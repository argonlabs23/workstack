# ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (STE) Rules Reference

This reference outlines the core writing rules specified by the **ASD-STE100** standard (Issue 8/9). The goal of ASD-STE100 is to eliminate ambiguity, simplify comprehension, and standardize technical and descriptive English for international audiences.

---

## 1. Word Choice & Vocabulary Rules

1. **Use Approved Words Only**:
   - Use words only as specified in the STE dictionary (approved spelling, part of speech, and defined meaning).
   - Do not use non-approved synonyms (e.g., use `start` instead of `commence`, `begin`, or `initiate`).
2. **One Meaning per Word**:
   - Words must only be used in their approved sense. For example, `close` is approved only as a verb ("close the valve"), not as an adjective ("near").
3. **One Part of Speech per Word**:
   - Words cannot switch parts of speech arbitrarily (e.g., if `oil` is defined as a noun, do not use it as a verb like "to oil the engine"; use "apply oil to the engine").
4. **Technical Names (TNs) & Technical Verbs (TVs)**:
   - Industry-specific or project-specific terms (hardware components, specific manufacturing processes) are permitted as Technical Names (TNs) or Technical Verbs (TVs) if they fit strict criteria and do not conflict with approved general dictionary words.

---

## 2. Nouns & Noun Clusters

1. **Limit Noun Cluster Length**:
   - Do not use noun clusters of more than **3 words** (e.g., "flight deck door lock" is acceptable; "flight deck door lock safety pin" must be rephrased as "safety pin for the flight deck door lock").
2. **Clarify Multi-Word Nouns**:
   - Use hyphens or prepositions to make the relationship between words explicit.
3. **Use Specific Nouns**:
   - Avoid vague nouns like `facility`, `system`, `apparatus`, `device`, or `thing` when a specific technical name exists.

---

## 3. Verbs, Voice & Tenses

1. **Use Active Voice**:
   - Write instructions and descriptions in active voice whenever possible.
   - *Non-compliant (Passive)*: "The test button must be pressed by the operator."
   - *Compliant (Active)*: "Press the test button."
2. **Use Simple Tenses**:
   - Use only simple infinitive, simple present, simple past, and simple future (`will`).
   - Avoid continuous/progressive tenses (`is running`, `was testing`) and perfect tenses (`has executed`, `had been opened`).
3. **Imperative Mood for Instructions**:
   - Start procedural steps with an imperative action verb ("Turn", "Disconnect", "Remove", "Check").
4. **Avoid Gerunds**:
   - Do not use "-ing" forms of verbs as nouns or adjectives unless explicitly approved as a Technical Name.
5. **Auxiliary Verbs**:
   - Restrict modal auxiliary verbs to `can`, `must`, `may`, and `will`. Avoid `should`, `could`, `would`, and `might`.

---

## 4. Sentence Length & Structure

1. **Strict Sentence Length Limits**:
   - **Procedural Sentences (Instructions)**: Maximum **20 words**.
   - **Descriptive Sentences (Explanations)**: Maximum **25 words**.
2. **One Instruction / One Idea per Sentence**:
   - Do not combine multiple actions or independent thoughts into complex sentences.
   - Break compound sentences joined by "and", "or", or "which" into short separate sentences.
3. **Conditional Clauses First**:
   - Place conditions or prerequisites BEFORE the action statement.
   - *Non-compliant*: "Turn off the switch if the red LED turns on."
   - *Compliant*: "If the red LED turns on, turn off the switch."

---

## 5. Paragraph Structure

1. **Paragraph Length Limit**:
   - Maximum **6 sentences** per paragraph.
2. **Single Topic**:
   - Each paragraph must cover only one clear topic or sequence step.
3. **Topic Sentence First**:
   - Start descriptive paragraphs with a clear statement of the topic.

---

## 6. Procedural vs. Descriptive Writing

- **Procedural Writing (Step-by-step instructions)**:
  - Keep sentences under 20 words.
  - Use imperative verbs.
  - Number each sequential step.
  - List items vertically if there are more than two.
- **Descriptive Writing (Background, explanations, descriptions)**:
  - Keep sentences under 25 words.
  - Use simple present tense.
  - Define components before explaining their operation.

---

## 7. Warnings, Cautions & Notes

1. **Clear Hierarchy**:
   - **WARNING**: Risk of injury or death to personnel.
   - **CAUTION**: Risk of damage to equipment or data.
   - **NOTE**: Essential background information.
2. **Placement**:
   - Place WARNINGS and CAUTIONS immediately BEFORE the affected step.
3. **Explicit Action**:
   - Clearly state the danger, the consequence, and the specific action required to avoid it.

---

## 8. Punctuation & Formatting

1. **Use Simple Punctuation**:
   - Rely primarily on periods (full stops), commas, hyphens, and colons.
   - Avoid semicolons (`;`) and em-dashes (`—`); split into separate sentences instead.
2. **Serial Commas (Oxford Comma)**:
   - Use serial commas in lists of three or more items ("A, B, and C").
3. **Hyphenation**:
   - Use hyphens for compound adjectives preceding a noun ("high-pressure valve").

---

## 9. Numbers & Measurements

1. **Numerical Digits**:
   - Use Arabic numerals (`1`, `2`, `10`) for dimensions, tolerances, quantities, and step numbers.
2. **Standard Units**:
   - Use standard SI or customary units consistently with appropriate approved abbreviations (`mm`, `kg`, `V`, `psi`).
